# Week 2026-W39

## Summary
This week the job hunt became fully automated end-to-end: Job Tracker's scout now scores and prioritizes postings across multiple sources, job-mail triages replies and drafts follow-ups, and both feed weekly and morning digests over Telegram. Alongside that, a new devlog project and site-watch service were built to keep the whole automation stack observable and reliable overnight, catching failures, retries, and sleep/scheduling bugs. A large chunk of the week also went into hardening: dependency-security audits cut vulnerability counts sharply across job-tracker, nexus-grind and spidey-portfolio, a public Supabase table was found and closed, and CI (Postman/Newman, Playwright) was added to Job Tracker. The portfolio and CVs were retargeted around this new AI-automation work to match the direction of the job search itself.

## Wins
- Built and shipped an autonomous job-scouting pipeline (Jobs.cz + Labour Office + company career pages) with two-round AI scoring, self-auditing for underrating bias, and a review Inbox
- Cut AI scoring cost ~20x per posting and added per-feature daily budgets to keep automation running reliably on free-tier limits
- Designed and shipped devlog and site-watch, two new internal services providing nightly cross-repo code review, dependency-vulnerability reporting, and uptime/heartbeat monitoring for scheduled jobs
- Found and remediated a publicly readable Supabase table and an unapplied RLS migration in a production mobile app
- Reduced dependency-audit findings sharply across three projects (e.g. 73→4, 19→6) with no critical issues remaining
- Added automated API and UI test coverage (Postman/Newman, Playwright) running in CI on every push

## LinkedIn draft
This week I turned my job search into an automated pipeline. A scout now scans job boards every morning, scores postings with AI, and tailors my CV per posting; replies land in Telegram with one-tap actions to update my tracking board; and a weekly digest summarizes applications, replies, and results.

To keep it all reliable while running unattended overnight, I built a small internal devlog and monitoring service that reviews the day's code, tracks dependency vulnerabilities, and alerts me if a scheduled job doesn't run. Along the way I also found and closed a publicly exposed database table in one of my apps, and cut security-audit findings sharply across three projects.

It's been a good reminder that the tooling you build to solve your own problem is often the most honest test of your skills.
